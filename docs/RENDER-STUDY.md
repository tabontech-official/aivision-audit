## Analysis and decisions (written summary required by B.10.1)

### The four questions

1. **What percentage of audits render?** 100% today — the render is
   unconditional. All 55 renders succeeded with a local browser installed
   (before this study the local browser was missing, so production-shaped
   environments were silently rendering 0% while *attempting* 100%).
2. **What percentage actually benefit?** 24% by the pipeline's existing
   ">20% richer HTML" rule. But see below — that rule is itself misleading.
3. **Render cost:** mean **8.8 s**, p95 **16.3 s** per audit. This is the
   single largest time and memory cost in the pipeline.
4. **Which static signals best predict a genuinely needed render?**
   **`staticWordCount` alone is the dominant predictor.** Every true
   client-shell in the sample (spotify 0 words, chatgpt 0, claude.ai 0,
   twitch 0, canva 25) had a near-empty static body, including apps whose
   mount-point id matches no known list. Mount-point emptiness and JS-framework
   markers confirm but never contradict it. Script counts and text/HTML ratio
   are weak on their own.

### The >20%-HTML-length "benefit" rule is wrong in both directions

Measured word counts show two failure modes of the existing rule:

- **False benefit (ad bloat):** variety.com (+224 KB, +457 words on a
  2,644-word page), tmz.com (+167 KB, **−27 words**), php.net (+139 KB,
  **±0 words**), kinsta, caniuse, discord. JS injected ads/trackers/consent
  frames — bytes grew, auditable content did not. Rendering these is the bulk
  of the 76% waste.
- **Missed real hydration:** canva.com went 25 → 713 words but HTML grew
  < 20% (the shell was already script-heavy), so the rule discarded a render
  that genuinely mattered.

Consequently the sweep was re-scored against a **content-gain** metric
(`renderedWords > staticWords × 1.5 + 30`) and the decision module gained the
dominant signal: `staticWordCount < 30 → +50`.

### Re-scored sweep (with the near-empty-body signal, content-gain metric)

| Threshold | Skip rate | Missed content-gain | Renders kept that gained nothing |
|---|---|---|---|
| 30 | 84% | brooklinen, kyliecosmetics, miro, skims | 6 |
| **40 (chosen)** | **85%** | brooklinen, kyliecosmetics, miro, skims | 5 |
| 50 | 87% | + same 4 | 4 |

**Chosen default: 40** (`RENDER_DECISION_THRESHOLD` overrides without a
deploy).

**The four accepted misses are safe misses.** brooklinen (571 static words),
kyliecosmetics, miro (1,060), skims (273) all carry full titles, headings and
substantial static content — their audits remain valid; what they lose is
lazy-loaded product-grid/App-shell text. The *unsafe* miss — a blank report —
cannot occur: any page with < 30 static words scores ≥ 50 and renders, and the
pipeline's safety valve additionally renders whenever static extraction comes
back with no title, no h1 and near-zero words.

**Remaining waste is deliberate caution:** tiny-but-legitimate static pages
(example.com, lite.duckduckgo) render unnecessarily because they fall under
the 30-word floor. Rare in real audits and errs in the safe direction.

### Screenshot finding (B.10.3)

`Report.screenshotUrl` is stored and passed to `ReportView` as a prop but
**rendered nowhere** — no `<img>` exists in the report UI or anywhere else.
It is decorative and currently invisible. Decision per the B.10.3 table:
captured only when a render happens anyway, skipped otherwise, never a reason
to launch a browser. The screenshot was **not** the reason RENDERING was
unconditional — the extraction-richness check was.

### Projected impact

At an 85% skip rate, mean pipeline time drops by ~7.5 s per audit
(0.85 × 8.8 s) and peak memory by 200–400 MB for skipped audits, which is what
makes a pay-per-use hosted browser (B.10.4) economically sensible.
